**Literature Phase**  
- Catalogued `childhoodbalancedpublic_original.csv` (14,443 respondents × 718 columns) and confirmed the key items we need: retrospective purity-culture exposure during ages 0–12 and 13–18, outcome anchors such as `I love myself (2l8994l)` and `I am satisfied with my romantic relationships (hp9qz6f)`, plus moderators (current religious practice, monogamy preference, parental guidance/humor scales). The captures span −3…+3 Likert distributions with plenty of variation, so we can treat them as continuous indices.  
- Surveyed the literature and surfaced the authoritative anchors we’ll cite: Ortiz et al. (2023, *J. Psychol. Theol.*, DOI `10.1177/00916471231182734`) validates a purity-culture scale; Sawatsky et al. (2024, *Sociology of Religion*, DOI `10.1093/socrel/srae031`) and Wacks, Lazar, & Sommerfeld (2022, *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, DOI `10.1007/s10508-022-02494-2`) document how purity tropes and adult religiosity shape sexual/relational well-being; Fang, Fosco, & Feinberg (2024, *Dev. Psychopathol.*, DOI `10.1017/S0954579423001207`) shows longstanding protective effects of parental warmth. These together highlight the gap we can fill by leveraging this broad cross-religious sample and its rich developmental timing battery.  
- Hypotheses ready for PAP: (1) Greater purity-culture exposure (especially during 13–18) predicts lower adult romantic satisfaction and self-love, with the slope differing by current religious practice/monogamy preference (adults still embedded in a faith community may process the shame differently than those who have disaffiliated; Wacks et al. provides the theoretical anchor). (2) Positive parental guidance/humor buffers the negative purity-message → anxiety/self-love link, extending Fang et al.’s findings by showing how supportive parenting can inoculate against shame-based messaging even when the ideology was present. These hypotheses go beyond past work by exploiting the multi-faith, gender-inclusive, retrospective timing data this survey uniquely provides.
