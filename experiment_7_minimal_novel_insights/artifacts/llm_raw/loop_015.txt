**Literature Phase**
- **Dataset:** `childhoodbalancedpublic_original.csv` holds 14,443 cases with 718 columns; the median respondent is 28 years old, mostly cisgender US/UK/Canadian adults. There are ~51 columns tied to childhood sexual assault, the derived `CSA_score` (0–22) and dozens of severity/response indicators, while `mentalillnesssize` counts reported adult diagnoses (0–19); the childhood- and adult-culture seriousness items I plan to use are ordinal (0–3/4) and only answered by the ~2,100 survivors, so the modeling sample will be that subset.
- **Literature:** CSA has persistent effects on adult well-being (Manukrishnan & Bhagabati 2023, *Journal of Psychosexual Health*, DOI 10.1177/26318318231221948) and survivors’ PTSD/coping vary systematically with the social reactions they receive (Ullman & Filipas 2005, *Child Abuse & Neglect*, DOI 10.1016/j.chiabu.2005.01.005); more recent work also links post-disclosure responses to long-term mental health, showing adult cultural context matters (Newins & Wilson 2021, DOI 10.1093/med-psych/9780197523643.003.0003). These works anchor a gap around “how seriously the surrounding culture treats sexual abuse” at different life stages.
- **Hypothesis 1:** Within survivors, higher scores on the childhood “household culture took sexual assault seriously” item will be associated with lower `mentalillnesssize`, even after adjusting for `CSA_score`, age, gender category, and country, because serious early responses are theorized to reduce the PTSD/coping load emphasized by Ullman & Filipas (2005) and thus might leave survivors with fewer lasting diagnoses.
- **Hypothesis 2:** Among the same survivors, the positive association between CSA severity and `mentalillnesssize` will be weaker when the adult culture (post-19) is perceived as taking sexual assault seriously; drawing on Newins & Wilson (2021), we treat adult-cultural seriousness as a buffering social-reaction context that can moderate early trauma’s imprint.
- **Next Steps:** I’ll now draft the pre-analysis plan that freezes the survivor sample, defines how I will derive/clean the seriousness and outcome scores, records covariates (CSA severity, demographics), specifies the regression form (e.g., count model for `mentalillnesssize` plus an interaction term for Hypothesis 2), and lays out missing-data rules and robustness checks before moving into analysis.
