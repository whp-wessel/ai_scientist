**Literature Update**
- Scoped `childhoodbalancedpublic_original.csv` (14,443 respondents × 718 columns) to map the childhood and adult batteries: the “*people expected gendered norms of you that you hated*” items at ages 0–12 and 13–18 are crisp −3…+3 scales that capture conflict with imposed gender roles, while adult attachment indicators (`Avoidant _attachmentstyle`, `Anxious _attachmentstyle`, `Disorganized _attachmentstyle`, `Secure _attachmentstyle`) plus affect proxies (e.g., `I tend to suffer from anxiety (npvfh98)-neg`, `I tend to suffer from depression (wz901dj)`, `I love myself (2l8994l)`) supply the outcomes; positive family climate is measured via the “family/culture had hilarious joking…” items and “you felt unconditionally loved” (both age windows), giving us in-dataset moderators that are conceptually distinct from purity-culture messaging.
- Grounded a novel question in the literature: childhood gender-role conflict is linked to worse adult well-being, so we will test whether higher scores on the gendered-norm conflict items predict insecure attachment (and elevated anxiety/depression) even after adjusting for demographics, religion, purity-culture teaching, and abuse exposures. Peer-reviewed work such as Blazina & Watkins (1996, *J. Counseling Psychology*, DOI:10.1037/0022-0167.43.4.461) and Courtenay (2000, *Soc. Sci. & Med.*, DOI:10.1016/S0277-9536(99)00390-1) documents that gender role conflict harms psychological well-being, making this a literature-extending test in a broader, balanced sample beyond college men.
- Proposed a second literature-extending hypothesis: the negative imprint of gendered-norm conflict on adult attachment/mental health is buffered by positive childhood experiences (family humor, unconditional love), echoing the protective-dose findings of Bethell et al. (2019, *JAMA Pediatrics*, DOI:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2019.3007) but refining them to interaction terms that land directly on attachment and internalizing symptoms.
- With this scoping done, we are ready to pre-register the PAP: next loop will operationalize the two hypothesis tests, fix covariates (age, gender/cis status, race/ethnicity, religiosity, CSA_score, parental abuse scales), specify coding (e.g., treat gender-norm conflict as continuous, attachment indicators as logistic outcomes, anxiety/depression composites as standardized scores), and outline the planned survey-aware regressions/robustness checks; once that PAP text is frozen we can move into the analysis phase.
