Packaged the frozen purity-culture findings into the Tectonic manuscript so the PDF, PGFPlots figure, and reproducibility assets are ready for release.

**Key Results**
- The adolescent (13–18) purity × parental-support interaction for self-love is β = −0.040 (p = 0.005, Cohen’s d ≈ −0.022), and the simple-slopes block in `tables/simple_slopes.json:6` shows the purity slope jumps from ≈0 at low support to ≈−0.081 at high support, meaning higher parental support sharpens rather than buffers the purity penalty (`tables/regression_results.csv:4`).
- Romantic satisfaction mirrors that sharpening (β = −0.0376, p = 0.037, d ≈ −0.017) even though parental support keeps a large positive main effect, so the counterintuitive moderation holds across both well-being anchors (`tables/regression_results.csv:16`).
- Early-childhood purity (ages 0–12) is modestly protective for anxiety (β = −0.1067, p = 0.002) and shows no support interaction, suggesting the earliest window behaves differently than adolescence (`tables/regression_results.csv:29`).
- Gender-minority slope differences remain small and imprecise (difference ≈0.067, SE ≈0.059 for self-love and ≈0.068, SE ≈0.072 for romantic satisfaction), so purity effects do not appear systematically harsher for gender-minority respondents even though their main disadvantage is sizable (`tables/simple_slopes.json:39`, `tables/simple_slopes.json:76`).

**Limitations & Open Questions**
- Parent-support reliability is still modest (ω ≈ 0.56), so disaggregating guidance versus humor or collecting richer family-climate data is needed before claiming anything beyond the composite-level interaction (`analysis/analysis_notes.md:120`).
- The negative moderation contradicts the preregistered buffering story; qualitative follow-up could reveal whether highly involved parents doubled down on purity norms, producing the steeper adult self-love/romantic-satisfaction slopes (`analysis/analysis_notes.md:125`, `analysis/analysis_notes.md:137`).
- Gender-minority moderation remains null even as minority status has a large main effect, so doctrinal nuance or intersectional axes (race, class, religiosity) may be required to detect any compounded stress (`analysis/analysis_notes.md:130`, `analysis/analysis_notes.md:138`).
- The work is cross-sectional and retrospective, which leaves open recall bias and whether parental support simply marks families that enforced purity more tightly, so causal claims should stay cautious (`analysis/analysis_notes.md:138`).
