**Key Findings**
- Hypothesis 1 still shows a negative purity×support interaction for self-love and romantic satisfaction (self-love `β=-0.040`, romantic `β=-0.0376`, both p<0.04, Cohen’s d ≈ -0.02) alongside a strong positive main slope for support, so higher parental involvement steepens the purity penalty (`tables/regression_results.csv:2-17`).
- Adolescent anxiety is modestly lower when childhood purity (0–12) is high (`β=-0.1067`, p=0.002) and displays no registered support interaction, indicating the counterintuitive moderation is specific to adolescent outcomes (`tables/regression_results.csv:26-29`).
- Gender-minority interaction terms remain null while minority status retains large main disadvantages (self-love `β≈-0.34`, anxiety `β≈0.13`), so purity messaging does not systematically worsen well-being for gender minorities in this sample (`tables/regression_results.csv:40-61`).
- Registered sensitivity slices (no current religion, added trauma/depression controls, trans/nonbinary subsamples) preserve the pattern or widen intervals without overturning it, confirming the robustness story (`tables/regression_results_no_current_religion.csv:2-13`, `tables/regression_results_with_trauma_controls.csv:2-13`, `tables/gender_minority_subgroups.csv:2-25`).
