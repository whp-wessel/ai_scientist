**Literature Phase**
- `childhoodbalancedpublic_original.csv` (≈14.4k rows × 718 columns) ties demographics and adult well-being (self-reported anxiety/depression/ happiness) to childhood socialization (purity-culture teachings, parental guidance/humor, media monitoring, emotional abuse, etc.), so we can model how early environments interact to shape current mental health.
- Dew et al. (2008, DOI:10.1007/s10578-007-0093-2) review how certain religious/spiritual experiences correlate with adolescent psychiatric symptoms, Odgers & Jensen (2020, DOI:10.1111/jcpp.13190) show that digital technology’s links to adolescent mental health are small but highlight its role as a potential social resource, and Jung (2021, DOI:10.1080/09638237.2021.1875411) finds that positive reappraisal moderates the childhood emotional-abuse → adult mental-health association; these findings point to moderators worth testing here.
- Hypothesis 1: Among respondents who report being taught a purity culture (scale `taught a purity culture...` or the age-specific versions), greater childhood internet/computer exposure (`during ages *0-12*` and `*13-18*` internet/computer time) will be associated with lower current anxiety/depression, capturing a buffer effect of digital exposure on the psychological cost of punitive religious messaging (extends Dew et al. 2008 and Odgers & Jensen 2020).
- Hypothesis 2: The positive link between childhood parental verbal/emotional abuse (`during ages *0-12*`: parents were verbally/emotionally abusive, etc.) and adult distress (anxiety/depression/happiness scales) will be weakened when those same respondents report that parents also provided useful guidance or a humorous, supportive family culture (`Your parents gave useful guidance`, “family/culture had hilarious joking…”), operationalizing Jung 2021’s positive-reappraisal moderator through observable parental behaviors.
