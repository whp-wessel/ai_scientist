- Analytic sample details (N=14,400, 8.3% gender-minority) and the two-item parent-support reliability of 0.56—below the usual 0.70 threshold—are recorded in `outputs/sample_summary.json:2` and `outputs/sample_summary.json:17`.

- Hypothesis 1 results show that high adolescence (13–18) purity is linked to lower romantic satisfaction (coef = −0.11, p = 0.009, d≈−0.05; `tables/regression_results.csv:6`) while parental support boosts self-love (coef = 0.21, p<0.001, d≈0.11; `tables/regression_results.csv:3`). Yet the purity×support interaction is negative for both self-love (coef = −0.040, p = 0.005; `tables/regression_results.csv:4`) and romantic satisfaction (coef = −0.038, p = 0.037; `tables/regression_results.csv:8`), so higher support widens rather than narrows the gap between low- and high-purity respondents—an unexpected pattern that the margin plot makes visible—and early purity (0–12) still shows a small but significant association with less favorable anxiety scores (coef = −0.107, p = 0.002, d≈−0.05; `tables/regression_results.csv:13`).
- Hypothesis 2 finds that gender-minority respondents report lower self-love (coef = −0.19, p = 0.001, d≈−0.10; `tables/regression_results.csv:16`), higher romantic satisfaction (coef = 0.52, p≪0.001, d≈0.23; `tables/regression_results.csv:24`), and lower anxiety (coef = −0.19, p≪0.001, d≈−0.09; `tables/regression_results.csv:32`), but none of the six purity×gender-minority interactions across the three outcomes is significant (`tables/regression_results.csv:17`, `:21`, `:25`, `:29`, `:33`, `:37`), so there is no evidence that purity culture has a disproportionately adverse association for the gender-minority subgroup in this sample.
