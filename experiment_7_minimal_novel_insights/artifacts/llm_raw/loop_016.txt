**Hypotheses**
- Adult culture (age 19+) that takes sexual assault seriously should show independent buffering of long-term mental-health outcomes even after adjusting for how seriously the childhood household treated the assault—building on the literature about how social reactions influence recovery trajectories (Ullman, S. E. 1996. *Do Social Reactions to Sexual Assault Victims Vary by Support Provider?* *Violence and Victims* 11(2):143–157; DOI 10.1891/0886-6708.11.2.143).
- Positive, protective experiences such as “parents gave useful guidance” may moderate or attenuate the negative association between purity-culture socialization (abstinence/virginity teaching) and adult relationship/self-esteem outcomes, in line with the protective-and-compensatory experiences framework (Bethell CD, Jones J, Gombojav N, Linkenbach J, Sege R. 2019. *Positive Childhood Experiences and Adult Mental and Relational Health in a Statewide Sample*. *JAMA Pediatrics* 173(11):e193007; DOI 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2019.3007).
